Websites, too

Dusk for Chrome follows your browser's light or dark setting and darkens compatible sites that have no dark mode. It works without the Mac app.

  • Designed to preserve photo and video colours; switch it off for sites that look wrong.
  • Use the popup to control individual sites. Available options depend on your installed version.
  • Automatic detection does not remember visits in Incognito windows. Settings you explicitly save in the popup may remain in Chrome.
  • It works by itself. The Mac app is not required.

What Dusk cannot do

  • macOS does not let one standard Mac app stay dark while the rest is light. Dusk can keep such an app light, but not dark.
  • Some apps, such as Calculator or Minecraft, have one fixed look. Nothing can change that, and Dusk says so.
  • When you choose Always Light, the direct-download edition adds or removes a standard macOS preference for that app. Separately, optional sync changes one documented theme value in supported apps' settings files and keeps a backup.
  • Pages that use colour to carry meaning, such as charts and maps, can look wrong when darkened. Switch Dusk off for that site.

Privacy depends on the edition

The Mac app can check its update feed; you can turn automatic checks off. The Chrome extension processes appearance locally and may re-fetch a page's images to inspect their colours, without cookies or a referrer. Read the direct-download and Chrome privacy policy for details.
